QA corrections & methodology notes (click to expand)
QA corrections (Opus review, July 2026): (1) Lithuania rebuilt to the athlete sports-activity model per Sodra: income tax 15% up to €253,065.60 (120 VDU) then 20%, with foreign players exempt from social security and local players paying 22.5% on 90% of the payment; total club cost = gross. (2) Turkey — added the foreign-player SGK exemption: EU/UK players (bilateral treaty) are exempt from Turkish social security; US players are not (no treaty). Income tax is identical for all. FX updated to 53.6 TRY/€, brackets TRY 190k/400k/1.5M/5.3M, SGK cap ~€66.5k/yr. (3) France — confirmed there is no social-charge exclusion for foreign players signing directly with a French club (A1/posting does not apply to direct hires); the Impatriate regime cuts income tax only (30–50%), employer charges unchanged. (4) Israel new-immigrant caps the exemption at NIS 1M. (5) Spain resident regimes use a progressive scale. (6) France — income tax and CEHR are now computed on taxable income (gross − employee SS − the 10% professional-expense allowance, capped €14,430) instead of raw gross; the previous version overstated French income tax by roughly a third at multi-million-euro salaries. The Impatriate regime is updated to the Finance Law 2024 reform — 50% exemption for 5 years for arrivals from Jan 2024 onward — replacing the earlier 30%/8-year modeling, which reflected pre-2024 rules. (7) France social security is now a capped two-tier model (uncapped CSG/CRDS/maladie/famille + a PASS tier €48,060 + an AGIRC-ARRCO tier to 8×PASS €384,480) instead of a flat rate — confirmed applicable to professional basketball via the LNB's Convention Collective, for local and foreign players alike, with no sport-specific reduced regime. (8) Spain — Catalonia's top rate corrected from 54% to 50%; the 54% figure was actually Valencia's and the two had been incorrectly merged into one option. Valencia, Andalucía (Málaga), Canary Islands, and Región de Murcia added as separate regions. The Basque Country (Baskonia/Álava, Bilbao/Bizkaia) is now modeled as its own self-contained foral scale (23%–49%) rather than reusing the common-regime brackets with a different top rate, since foral IRPF is not summed with the state scale. Spain SS is now capped at €61,214.40/yr (2026 PASS-equivalent) plus the small uncapped "cuota de solidaridad" tiers introduced for high earners. (9) Greece verified against multiple 2026 sources: the calculator's 9/20/26/34/39/44% bracket scale (top rate starting at €60k) is the current, reformed scale under Law 5246/2025 — several third-party sites still show the older pre-reform 9/22/28/36/44% scale with the 44% threshold at €40k, which is now out of date. Employee EFKA corrected from 13.37% to 13.87% to match the confirmed 2026 rate; employer 21.79% and the €93,143/yr cap were already correct. (10) Greece — added an optional signing/transfer/renewal bonus field: under Law 4646/2019 art.76, a contract bonus over €40,000 (explicitly covering basketball, per AADE circular Ε.2026/2020) is taxed at a flat 22% final withholding instead of the ordinary 9–44% scale, and is not subject to EFKA. Applies equally to local and foreign players since it depends on payment structure, not nationality. (11) Turkey verified against the official GİB 2026 tariff: the calculator's wage-income brackets (15/20/27/35/40% at TRY 190k/400k/1.5M/5.3M) match exactly, confirming several third-party sites showing lower thresholds (e.g. TRY 110k/230k/870k/3M) are using stale pre-2026 figures. No nationality-based income-tax exemption found for foreigners, non-residents, or new taxpayers under Geçici Madde 72 — it applies identically regardless of residency. Added a clarification that press-reported "net" salaries for high earners typically reflect only the in-season 20% stopaj, not the true final net after the mandatory annual declaration (which applies above the ~TRY 5.3M threshold) — a large gap between the two is normal, not evidence of a hidden exemption. Also noted: Turkey's Constitutional Court (3 June 2026) struck down the rule that a club's withheld tax had to be remitted before a player could credit it against their declared liability. (12) Italy verified: the calculator's 23/33/43% brackets already matched the correct, current 2026 scale (2026 Budget Law cut the middle bracket from 35% to 33%) — several third-party sites still show the old 35%. Employee INPS fixed to a two-tier structure (9.19% up to €56,224/yr, then 10.19% up to the €122,295 cap) instead of a flat 9.19%. (13) Italy Impatriati — CORRECTED after further review: the initial rebuild wrongly applied an athlete-specific €1,000,000/yr income floor (with age-20 and 0.5% levy requirements) sourced from art.16 of D.Lgs.147/2015 — but that article was repealed by D.Lgs.209/2023 for anyone transferring Italian residence from 2024 onward, and only survives for players who signed contracts by 31 Dec 2023. The regime now correctly reflects the current D.Lgs.209/2023 art.5 rules that apply to athletes the same as any other qualifying worker: 50% exemption up to a €600,000/yr cap (not a floor) — income above €600k is fully taxed at ordinary rates with no cliff or gap. No age requirement, no 0.5% levy in the current regime. (14) Israel — added a third, previously-missing regime: foreign athlete (flat 25%), under the 1998 Foreign Athlete tax regulations. This is separate from both the standard resident scale (up to 50%) and the new-immigrant/oleh-chadash track (which is about becoming an Israeli resident, more relevant to a returning national than a genuine import). Foreign athletes who remain non-residents get a flat 25% rate plus sharply reduced National Insurance (~2% vs. the resident rate), for up to 4 years — this is what produces the commonly-cited ~1.25x gross-to-net multiplier for imports vs. ~2x for local players. (15) Germany — the income tax formula was using stale §32a EStG bracket boundaries and coefficients from an earlier tax year (e.g. the 42%-bracket threshold was €68,500 instead of the correct 2026 value €69,879, with outdated zone-4/5 formula constants). Corrected to the current 2026 tariff. Also fixed the Pflegeversicherung (care insurance) employer share to the confirmed-fixed 1.7% (was 1.8%). Confirmed via multiple sources: Germany has no special regime for athletes or foreign players — once a player signs and relocates, they're taxed as an ordinary employee regardless of nationality; the only nationality-relevant nuance is a narrow §50a withholding for one-off foreign appearances without German residency, not relevant to a season-long contract.
(16) Serbia: the SS contribution cap was corrected from ~€90,000/yr to ~€74,940/yr — the old figure didn't match the official 2026 maximum monthly base (732,820 RSD, published by the PIO fund) converted at the current ~117.35 RSD/€ rate. The mandatory annual surtax (godišnji porez na dohodak građana) is now included directly in the tax calculation rather than left out as a warning-only caveat — it's genuinely material at basketball-level salaries: 10% on net income above roughly €46,354 (3× average annual salary), rising to 15% above about €139,000 total, modeled using the extra under-40 personal deduction that applies to local and foreign players alike (not just Art.15v users). Also corrected the "Art.15v" option's framing: the primary qualifying path for the 70% payroll-tax-base reduction is not having predominantly resided in Serbia for 24 months, with no hard age-40 cutoff on that main path — the under-40 condition only applies to a separate, narrower path for people returning from study abroad.
(17) Turkey withholding — real bug fixed: the "20%, income ≤ ~€99k" option was computing a flat 20% withholding for any salary size, with no cap actually enforced in the code — the €99k figure only lived in the label text. A €1M net target was incorrectly resolving to a €1.26M gross taxed at a flat 20%, when Turkish law requires the full progressive scale (up to 40%) via mandatory annual declaration above that threshold. Fixed: this option now automatically falls through to the same full-progressive calculation as the high-earner option once gross exceeds ~€98,881/yr, matching the confirmed 20%-stopaj mechanics discussed for the Mike James case. Net-input mode also correctly handles the ~€57k–€69k zone right at the threshold where crossing it makes tax worse (net drops) rather than better — unlike Italy's Impatriati cap, this creates an overlap (two valid gross solutions) rather than a gap, and the calculator automatically resolves to the cheaper, lower-gross one.
